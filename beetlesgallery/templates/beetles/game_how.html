Show me around the game

In 30 seconds

  1. Tap Play. Each beetle is either Identification (name it) or Similarity (how related are two beetles?).
  2. Stop at the rank you're sure of. A right tribe scores more than the same tribe with a guessed, wrong genus. Skipping costs almost nothing.
  3. Take your time. There is no timer: look things up in keys, papers or a reference collection, and pause whenever you like.
  4. Photo shows no useful characters (an underside, a leg)? Still answer as far as you can: even just the subfamily helps. Report only a photo that is broken: wrong name, a box that misses the beetle, blurry, not a beetle.
  5. Tap Exit when you're done.

Your daily goal adapts to how much you play, like a step goal on a fitness watch: beat it and tomorrow's goal goes up a little, fall short or take a day off and it eases down. It never drops below {{ goal_floor }} beetles.

You start with Similarity. Identification, and choosing between the two, unlock at level 2; new players get more Similarity, experts more Identification.

Scoring

1. Beetles we already know

Some beetles in the feed have an expert-checked name. You never know which ones, so treat every beetle as if it counts.

Identification (Name That Beetle)

Naming a beetle is the harder game, so it is worth {{ classify_weight }}× the points. You get partial credit: each rank you get right earns its points.

Similarity

The right answer earns more the finer the difference you had to spot:

{{ name|capfirst }}{{ pts|digit_groups }}

Two photos that look alike (same photographer, place or magnification) earn up to a quarter more. Partial credit here too: “same tribe” for two beetles of one genus is true, so it earns the tribe’s points; “same species” earns the genus’s points minus a little. Calling relatives strangers (or strangers relatives) loses 1 point per step.

2. Beetles nobody has checked yet

Here there is no right answer to compare with yet, so you earn points by agreeing with strong players:

  • Only players in the better half on known beetles count as judges, and proven experts for that part of the tree count the most.
  • Agreeing with players stronger than you earns nearly full credit; agreeing with weaker players earns very little.
  • If a stronger player disagrees with you, agreeing with lots of weaker players does not help.
  • The more strong players agree with you, the more you earn, up to {{ cap }}% of what the same answer would earn on a known beetle. It never costs you points.
  • You can also earn that straight away when you match what proven experts said about the beetle, or what the identification model says when it is sure and has a good record on that exact group. Not matching them costs nothing, and none of this counts towards your accuracy: only checked beetles do.

3. Scores keep up with the truth

When a curator later checks a beetle you answered, your answer is scored again against the real name, up or down, and it counts towards your accuracy and expertise. You'll see it on the game home under Checked since you played, with the photo and the real name. Points from agreement move as more people answer, whenever anyone finishes playing and every night.

4. Learning from mistakes

A known beetle you got wrong comes back after {{ retry_days }} days or more, marked “Seen before”. Getting it right then earns half points. Second tries help your score but not your accuracy, which only counts the first time you see a beetle.

5. Not sure?

Skipping or answering “Not sure” costs a tiny {{ unsure }} points, much less than a wrong guess. Your score never drops below zero.

Where your labels go

  • From level {{ proposal_level }}, your names for beetles nobody has checked yet go to the curators as suggestions. Curators decide.
  • Only real experts have their labels written to the database without review. You become one by proving yourself on beetles experts have already checked, in that exact taxon: {{ per_species }} images of every species in it (all of them, for a species with fewer), with at least {% widthratio trust_accuracy 1 100 %}% right. Small taxa need few answers, large ones many. You must also be among the most reliable players overall.
  • Then, when {{ min_experts }} or more such experts give the same species for a beetle with no name yet, and no proven player disagrees, it is written to the database. It stays marked unvalidated until a curator checks it.
  • Expertise is earned one taxon at a time (a subfamily, a tribe, a genus). You can be an expert on one genus and not on another, so some of your labels may skip review and others not. Being good in neighbouring taxa only makes your labels suggestions. Your expertise tree shows where you stand.
  • Expertise is checked again all the time, so it can be lost as well as earned.
  • Found a new species? If you name a species the gallery has no validated images of yet, and a curator later confirms you were right, you get a pop-up and a New species finder badge on your profile.

Leaderboard

The leaderboard ranks players by score, accuracy or beetles seen, all time or this week. Tap a name to see that player. The specialists board ranks players inside one subfamily, tribe or genus.
