How the game works

Show me around the game

In 30 seconds

  1. Tap Play. Each beetle is one of four games: Similarity (how related are two beetles?), Odd One Out (which beetle doesn't belong?), Find Them All (select every beetle of one group) or Naming (name it).
  2. Stop at the rank you're sure of. A correct tribe scores more than the same tribe with a guessed, wrong genus. Skipping costs almost nothing.
  3. Take your time. There is no timer: look things up in keys, papers or a reference collection, and pause whenever you like.
  4. A photo must show a good part of the beetle.
  5. Tap Exit when you're done.
Your daily goal, and when games unlock

Your daily goal adapts to how much you play, like a step goal on a fitness watch: beat it and tomorrow's goal goes up a little, fall short or take a day off and it eases down. It never drops below {{ goal_floor }} beetles.

You start with Similarity. Odd One Out, and choosing your game, unlock at level {{ odd_level }}, Find Them All at level {{ select_level }} and Naming at level {{ identify_level }}. New players get more Similarity, higher levels more Naming.

New players start with the big groups: only the subfamily at first. The tribe opens after {{ rank_steps.tribe }} beetles, the genus after {{ rank_steps.genus }} and the species after {{ rank_steps.species }}, in every game. From level {{ ranks_all_level }} every rank is open.

1. Beetles we already know

Some beetles in the feed have an expert-checked name. You never know which ones, so treat every beetle as if it counts.

  • Naming: each rank you get correct earns points, and Naming pays the most. A wrong rank costs more than a correct one earns, so name a rank only when you’re at least {{ confidence }}% sure.
  • Similarity: the finer the difference you spot, the more you earn. A cautious answer that is true still earns something; calling two beetles closer relatives than they are costs points.
  • Odd One Out: all but one beetle share a name; bigger grids hide two to four odd ones, and you pick that many. A wrong pick costs points, so when you’re unsure, Skip: it earns {{ odd_skip }}.
  • Find Them All: tap every beetle of the group named under the photos. A wrong tap costs points; leaving one out costs nothing.
  • Both grids grow as you get better: from 4 to 25 beetles (in Odd One Out, then with two, three and four odd ones). Bigger grids are worth more. A bad photo in a grid? Open it with its corner button and flag it.
    • {% if difficulty_spread %}
  • In Naming and Similarity, harder beetles are worth more: up to {{ difficulty_spread }}% more on the hardest.
    • {% endif %}

2. Beetles nobody has checked yet

Here there is no correct answer to compare with yet, so you earn points by agreeing with strong players:

  • The more players stronger than you (or Naming experts, or a confident identification model) agree with you, the more you earn — up to {{ cap }}% of what the same answer would earn on a known beetle. Agreeing with weaker players earns very little.
  • A name stronger players disagree with takes points back, but an answer here never costs you points overall, and none of it counts towards your accuracy: only checked beetles do.

3. Scores keep up with the truth

When a curator later checks a beetle you answered, your answer is scored again against the real name, up or down, and it counts towards your accuracy and expertise. You'll see it on the game home under Reviewed by curators, with the photo and the real name. Points from agreement move as more people answer, whenever anyone finishes playing and every night.

4. Learning from mistakes

A known beetle you got wrong comes back in a later session, first in an easier game, marked “Seen before”. Getting it correct then earns half points. Second tries help your score but not your accuracy, which only counts the first time you see a beetle.

After each answer you see every beetle’s names. Those beetles come back too, in a later session at least {{ reveal_hours }} hour{{ reveal_hours|pluralize }} on, usually in another game, so you learn them by playing. They earn full points but don’t count towards your accuracy or expertise: those only count beetles you name unseen. Once you haven’t seen a beetle for {{ recall_days }} day{{ recall_days|pluralize }}, naming it counts again.

5. Not sure?

Skipping or answering “Not sure” costs a tiny {{ unsure }} points, much less than a wrong guess. Your score never drops below zero.

Focus lets you play only one subfamily, tribe or genus: beetles named in it, and beetles nobody has named yet that IBBI-AI places there. Change it any time on the unlocks page.

Where your labels go
  • From level {{ proposal_level }}, your names for beetles nobody has checked yet go to the curators as suggestions. Curators decide.
  • Only Naming experts have their labels written to the database without review. You become one by proving yourself on beetles curators have already checked, in that exact taxon, in Naming and Find Them All: {{ per_species }} images each of at least {% widthratio children_share 1 100 %}% of its members (a genus’s species, a tribe’s genera, a subfamily’s tribes), with at least {% widthratio trust_accuracy 1 100 %}% correct. Small taxa need few answers, large ones many. You must also be among the most reliable players overall.
  • Then, when {{ min_experts }} or more such Naming experts give the same species for a beetle with no name yet, and no other Naming expert disagrees, it is written to the database. It stays marked unvalidated until a curator checks it.
  • Expertise is earned one taxon at a time (a subfamily, a tribe, a genus). You can be a Naming expert on one genus and not on another, so some of your labels may skip review and others not. Being good in neighbouring taxa only makes your labels suggestions. Your expertise tree shows where you stand.
  • Telling a taxon’s beetles apart just as well, in Similarity and Odd One Out, makes you a Distinction expert there. It shows on your tree but doesn’t change where your labels go.
  • Expertise is checked again all the time, so it can be lost as well as earned.
  • Found a new species? If you name a species the gallery has no validated images of yet, and a curator later confirms you were correct, you get a pop-up and a New species finder badge on your profile.

Leaderboard

The leaderboard ranks players by score, accuracy or beetles seen: this week (it starts again every Monday), this month, or all time. Only the board's points start again; your level, expertise and badges never reset, and each week's top three are kept on their profiles. Tap a name to see that player. The specialists board ranks players inside one subfamily, tribe or genus.

Quick answers

Do I lose points if I flag a photo?
No. It goes to the curators and you get the next beetle.
What does skipping cost?
A tiny {{ unsure }} points in Similarity and Naming, much less than a wrong guess. In Odd One Out and Find Them All, skipping earns {{ odd_skip }}.
Is there a time limit?
No. Take your time, use keys, papers or a reference collection, and stop whenever you like.
When should I flag a photo?
When it doesn’t show a good part of the beetle, is blurry or dark, isn’t a beetle, or its box misses the beetle or frames the label. A clear photo of a good part of the beetle from an unusual side (from below, say) isn’t bad: name it as far as you can, even just the subfamily. Those answers show which views allow a name at which rank. A name that looks wrong is reported after you play, from the round’s answers in History.
Do other players affect my score?
Only on beetles nobody has checked yet: there you earn points when strong players agree with you, and you never lose any (more). On checked beetles only the real name counts.
What is an expert?
There are two kinds, each for one taxon. A Naming expert names its beetles reliably, proven on checked beetles across most of its members: their names for unchecked beetles there can go into the database without review (more). A Distinction expert tells its beetles apart just as reliably but may not know their names; it doesn’t unlock anything.
