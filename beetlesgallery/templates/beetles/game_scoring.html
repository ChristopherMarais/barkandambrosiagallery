How scoring works

Superusers only. Everything a player gains or loses, why, and the numbers in use right now. The aim: the most points go to answers that are correct and as precise as the player is sure of, so the game's labels can be trusted. Tune the numbers at the bottom; the checks tell you if a change would start rewarding guessing.

In short: a claim pays only when the player is at least {% widthratio v.GAME_POINTS_CONFIDENCE 1 100 %}% sure of it, a wrong claim costs {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }}× what it would earn, and unvalidated beetles score by agreement with stronger players. Each part below opens on its own; the contents jump straight to one.

Principles
  1. Claim only what you are sure of. Every claim (a rank named, a Similarity rung, an Odd One Out pick, a Find Them All tap) earns its points when correct and costs k = {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }} times them when wrong. So a claim pays on average only when the player is at least {% widthratio v.GAME_POINTS_CONFIDENCE 1 100 %}% sure of it; below that, stopping a rank earlier, a more cautious rung or leaving a beetle untapped earns more.
  2. Wrong is wrong. Anything claimed that isn't true is a mistake, however much else was right: "same genus" for beetles of two subfamilies is wrong, not partly correct. "Partly correct" is kept for answers that are true as far as they go and stop short.
  3. Precise beats vague, when correct: species is worth the most, then genus, tribe, subfamily, and each step deeper is worth more than the one before.
  4. Not knowing is cheap. A skip costs less than the smallest mistake (and earns a little in the grid games), so guessing never pays.
  5. The truth decides where we have it. On validated beetles answers are scored against the real name. Players are never told which beetles those are, so they have to try their best on every one.
  6. Elsewhere, agreement with strong players. On unvalidated beetles an answer earns points only when players with a better record (or Naming experts, or a model proven in that group) agree. A rank they dispute takes points back from the rest of the answer, which never goes below zero there, and it earns less than on validated beetles.
  7. Scores follow the truth over time. When a beetle is validated or its name corrected, every answer on it is re-scored, up or down: when the player leaves the game, every night, or with "Re-score everyone" below.
  8. Levels need reliability as well as points, so playing a lot is not enough to reach the top; being correct is.

Balance: is careful, precise play the best way to score?

The assessment behind the numbers (#530). Before it, a wrong rank after correct ones cost only 35% of its points, so naming one rank deeper paid from about 26% sure: guessing the species beat stopping at a known genus. In Similarity, “same genus” for beetles of two subfamilies counted as partly correct, and calling two beetles closer relatives than they are still earned points. Old grids from before the ladder would have gained up to ×1.5 on a re-score. Now one confidence rule prices every claim in every game, wrong claims are wrong, the steps get steeper toward species, and old grids keep what they earned.

How sure a player must be for a claim to pay

The chance of being right above which a claim earns more on average than not making it. Going deeper sits at the confidence setting on a middling beetle; hard beetles pay more and cost less, so they ask a little less, easy ones a little more. Answering at all also earns the point for taking part, against a skip.

Validated beetles: scored against the truth

Naming (name the beetle)

Points per rank named correctly: subfamily {{ v.GAME_POINTS_RANK.subfamily|floatformat:"-2" }}, tribe {{ v.GAME_POINTS_RANK.tribe|floatformat:"-2" }}, genus {{ v.GAME_POINTS_RANK.genus|floatformat:"-2" }}, species {{ v.GAME_POINTS_RANK.species|floatformat:"-2" }}, all times the Naming weight ×{{ v.GAME_POINTS_CLASSIFY_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}.

points = weight × ( Σ points of each correct rank − k × Σ points of each wrong rank )
k      = t ÷ (1 − t) = {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }}      t = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_CONFIDENCE|floatformat:"-2" }}   weight = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_CLASSIFY_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}

A rank below a wrong one is wrong too: naming the species claims every rank above it. Leaving a rank blank costs nothing, so a player names each rank only while at least t sure of it.

too close a tie:       P[true] − k × (P[said] − P[true])        e.g. "same genus" for one tribe  wrong
related, of strangers: − k × P[said]                                                          wrong

P = {{ pair_text }}
bonus = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_SIMILARITY_BONUS|floatformat:"-2" }}   k = {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }}

Photo likeness (0 to 1) counts what two photos share: photographer, institution, magnification, country, view. Alike photos are harder to tell apart, so they pay a little more.

The grid games grow with the player

Find Them All has 16 steps: 4, 9, 16 and 25 beetles at subfamily, then the same at tribe, genus and species. Odd One Out has 40: at each rank 4, 9, 16 and 25 beetles with one odd one, then 9, 16 and 25 with two, 16 and 25 with three, and 25 with four. A player goes up a step after {{ v.GAME_GRID_UP_AFTER }} good grids in a row and down one after a poor grid, and never past the ranks they have open. When the beetles for a step are short, the grid is built smaller, or at the nearest other rank, rather than not at all. Every point of a grid built on the ladder is times its size (grids from before it keep ×1, so a re-score doesn't inflate them): 4 beetles ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.4|floatformat:"-2" }}, 9 ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.9|floatformat:"-2" }}, 16 ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.16|floatformat:"-2" }}, 25 ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.25|floatformat:"-2" }}. A photo flagged as bad leaves the grid and counts for nothing; half of them flagged (or the odd one) ends the grid, unscored.

Odd One Out

worth   = odd_weight × P[how related the odd one is to the rest] × size   odd_weight = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}
correct = + worth
wrong   = − k × worth                                                   k = {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }}
skip    = + {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_SKIP|floatformat:"-2" }}

A pick pays only when the player is at least t sure of it, so a blind guess loses at every size (see the checks below). A pick on an unvalidated beetle is scored like a name on it (below): by agreement that it doesn't belong, never negative.

Find Them All

share  = select_weight × P[the grid's rank] × size ÷ validated members   select_weight = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_SELECT_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}
points = share × ( members tapped − k × non-members tapped ), never below − a perfect grid   k = {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }}
members left out cost nothing; taps on unvalidated beetles are recorded, not scored; skip = + {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_SKIP|floatformat:"-2" }}

Members are about a quarter to under half of a grid (4 beetles: 1–2, 9: 3–4, 16: 5–7, 25: 7–10) and validated non-members are never fewer, so tapping everything loses. A tap pays only when the player is at least t sure the beetle belongs; a member left out is the cautious answer. One wrong tap costs about what the same wrong claim costs in Similarity, and a grid can't lose more than a perfect one earns.

In every game

  • Taking part: every real answer earns +{{ v.GAME_POINTS_PARTICIPATION|floatformat:"-2" }} on top, so the score grows with play.
  • Skip in Naming and Similarity costs {{ v.GAME_POINTS_UNSURE|floatformat:"-2" }}; in Odd One Out and Find Them All it earns {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_SKIP|floatformat:"-2" }}.
  • Seen again: a beetle the player got wrong comes back to be learned and earns ×{{ v.GAME_POINTS_RETRY_FACTOR|floatformat:"-2" }}. Only the first time counts towards accuracy.
  • Reported: while a player's report on a beetle is open, their answers on it are held (no points either way).
  • Score is the running total, never below zero.

Unvalidated beetles: scored by agreement

Here nobody knows the answer yet, so a player earns points when stronger players agree with them. A rank they disagree with takes back k times what agreeing would earn, so a deep guess doesn't pay here either; an answer is never below zero in all, so honest answers on hard beetles are never punished.

for each rank the player named:
  judge weight  = 1                                        a Naming expert for that part of the tree
                = 1 / (1 + e^−(judge rating − your rating) / spread)   otherwise (spread = {{ v.GAME_RATER_SPREAD|floatformat:"-2" }})
  c             = (Σ weight agreeing − Σ weight disagreeing) / (Σ all weights + 1)      from −1 to +1
  points        = cap × P[rank] × c          when c ≥ 0                             cap = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_CONSENSUS_CAP|floatformat:"-2" }}
                = cap × P[rank] × k × c      when c < 0 (judges disagree)         k = {{ wrong_cost|floatformat:2 }}
if Naming experts (or a trusted model) name that rank:
  you match     points = max(points, reference_cap × P[rank])                       reference_cap = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_REFERENCE_CAP|floatformat:"-2" }}
  you don't     points = min(points, − k × reference_cap × P[rank])
answer = max(0, Σ points)   (Naming answers then × weight {{ v.GAME_POINTS_CLASSIFY_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }})
  • Judges are players with at least {{ v.GAME_RATER_MIN_JUDGED }} answers on validated beetles and a rating at or above the median. Weaker players never move anyone's points.
  • Agreeing with stronger players earns almost full credit; with weaker ones, very little. A stronger player who disagrees cancels out agreement from weaker ones, so a crowd of weak players can't outvote one strong one.
  • The "+1" in c means one judge alone never gives full credit: the more strong players agree, the closer to the cap.
  • A trusted model counts only when it is at least {{ v.GAME_REF_MODEL_MIN_CONFIDENCE|floatformat:"-2" }} sure of this beetle and has been correct {{ v.GAME_REF_MODEL_MIN_PRECISION|floatformat:"-2" }} of the time on at least {{ v.GAME_REF_MODEL_MIN_CHECKED }} of its sure calls in that same group.
  • These points move as more people answer, and are replaced by the real score once the beetle is validated. They never count towards accuracy or expertise.

Naming confidence: what a beetle is, and what it is not

Points are for players; this is about the beetles. Every answer on an unvalidated beetle is also evidence about its name, rank by rank. Evidence comes in two kinds.

What it is

  • Trusted at a rank: a Naming expert for that part of the tree supports the name and no trusted player disagrees. Taps alone never make a name trusted.
  • Curator tip on the Image Annotation page: the deepest rank that is trusted, or has at least {{ v.GAME_TIP_MIN_VOTES }} players and {{ v.GAME_TIP_MIN_SUPPORT|floatformat:"-2" }} support ("3 reliable players say genus Xyleborus"), flagged when it disagrees with the current label.
  • Written in: when {{ v.GAME_AUTO_APPLY_MIN_EXPERTS }} Naming experts give the same species for a beetle with no species name, it is written in as an Expert ID, still unvalidated for a curator to confirm. Otherwise names only reach a beetle through a curator.
  • The AI adds its own suggestion (shown separately); it becomes a reference for scoring only when proven in that group (see above).

A "not in" tip ("Players are confident it is not in genus Xyleborus", or "Naming experts and players…") appears once at least {{ v.GAME_TIP_MIN_NOT_VOTES }} players say so and they are at least {{ v.GAME_TIP_MIN_SUPPORT|floatformat:"-2" }} of everyone who spoke to that group either way. It helps a curator rule names out, and flags a current label that players reject.

What players see

  • After each answer: what players ranked above them said about that beetle, or that they are the first to name it. Only stronger players count, so newcomers learn from better players, not from each other.
  • Grid games: on validated beetles, the answer straight away: the odd one ringed with its name against the rest's ("Odd one: Xylosandrus · the rest: Xyleborus"); in Find Them All, members found, wrong taps and members missed. This shows what each beetle is and what it is not.
  • Round review and "Where I lost points": the real name of each validated beetle and, rank by rank, where an answer went wrong.
  • Reviewed by curators: when a beetle they answered is validated later, its real name and how their points moved.
  • Players are never told which beetles are validated before they answer, and never see the votes of players below them.

Reliability, levels and experts
  • Reliability (rating) is the lower end of a Wilson interval on how often the player is correct, rank by rank, the first time they see each validated beetle. A few lucky answers don't make anyone an authority: the rating rises only with many correct answers. A Find Them All grid counts once, as correct only when perfect.
  • Levels need points and a rating (none at levels 1–2, 35% at 3, rising to 92% at the top), so the top levels are kept only while answers stay good.
  • Naming experts in a taxon have named {{ v.GAME_TRUST_IMAGES_PER_SPECIES }} validated images each of at least {{ v.GAME_TRUST_CHILDREN_SHARE|floatformat:"-2" }} of its members (a genus's species, a tribe's genera, a subfamily's tribes; at least {{ v.GAME_TRUST_MIN_JUDGED }} answers in all) and been correct at least {{ v.GAME_TRUST_MIN_ACCURACY|floatformat:"-2" }} of the time, and are among the top {{ v.GAME_EXPERT_PERCENTILE|floatformat:"-2" }} of players by rating. Naming counts Naming answers and Find Them All grids: a member tapped is a correct name for its taxon, a wrong tap or a member left out a wrong one, and a grid counts once per taxon it showed, each beetle once per rank. Their agreeing labels go to curators as trusted suggestions; when two such experts agree on a beetle with no name, it is written in as an Expert ID, still unvalidated for a curator to confirm.
  • Distinction experts reach the same coverage and accuracy telling a taxon's members apart in Similarity and Odd One Out (each judged answer covers the members it showed). It is shown on their expertise tree and profile only: it never makes a label trusted, weighs as a judge or writes anything in.

Difficulty: which beetles a player sees, and what they are worth

Which beetles

Each beetle has a difficulty from 0 (easy) to 1 (hard): 1 − IBBI-AI's confidence, or else how often players get it wrong. Beetles are picked near the player's target:

target = start + per_batch × finished batches + skill_weight × rating     (at most max)
in Naming and Similarity, over the player's last {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_RECENT }} answers on validated beetles in that game:
  share correct < {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_EASE_BELOW|floatformat:"-2" }}   target − {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_EASE|floatformat:"-2" }}   (never below 0.05)
  share correct > {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_PUSH_ABOVE|floatformat:"-2" }}   target + {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_PUSH|floatformat:"-2" }}   (never above max)
start = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_START|floatformat:"-3" }}   per_batch = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_PER_ROUND|floatformat:"-3" }}   skill_weight = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_SKILL_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}   max = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_MAX|floatformat:"-2" }}

The rating moves slowly; the recent answers react within a few batches, so a player who struggles gets a breather and one who is doing well keeps being stretched.

What they are worth (Naming and Similarity)

p    = how hard the beetle is among all playable beetles when answered (0 easiest, 1 hardest; 0.5 if unknown)
m    = 1 + spread × (2p − 1)                                spread = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_DIFFICULTY_SPREAD|floatformat:"-2" }}
gain × m,   loss (skips too) × (2 − m)                       the point for taking part is not scaled

One factor for the whole answer, so a wrong answer never turns into a gain and every comparison between answers on one beetle stays as above. Odd One Out and Find Them All are priced by their own grid instead. Answers from before this have no p and keep ×1.
