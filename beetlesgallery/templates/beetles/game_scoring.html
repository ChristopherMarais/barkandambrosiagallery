How scoring works

Superusers only. Everything a player gains or loses, why, and the numbers in use right now. The aim: the most points go to answers that are correct and as precise as the player is sure of, so the game's labels can be trusted. Tune the numbers at the bottom; the checks tell you if a change would start rewarding guessing.

Principles

  1. Correct beats confident. Each rank named correctly earns points; a wrong rank costs. Going one rank further than you know earns less than stopping, so players stop where they are sure.
  2. Precise beats vague, when correct: species is worth the most, then genus, tribe, subfamily.
  3. Not knowing is cheap. A skip costs less than the smallest mistake (and earns a little in the grid games), so guessing never pays.
  4. The truth decides where we have it. On validated beetles answers are scored against the real name. Players are never told which beetles those are, so they have to try their best on every one.
  5. Elsewhere, agreement with strong players. On unvalidated beetles an answer earns points only when players with a better record (or Identification experts, or a model proven in that group) agree. It never costs points there, and it earns less than on validated beetles.
  6. Scores follow the truth over time. When a beetle is validated or its name corrected, every answer on it is re-scored, up or down: when the player leaves the game, every night, or with "Re-score everyone" below.
  7. Levels need reliability as well as points, so playing a lot is not enough to reach the top; being correct is.

The grid games grow with the player

Odd One Out and Select all each have 12 steps: 4, 9 and 16 beetles at subfamily, then the same at tribe, genus and species. A player goes up a step after {{ v.GAME_GRID_UP_AFTER }} good grids in a row and down one after a poor grid, and never past the ranks they have open. When the beetles for a step are short, the grid is built smaller, or at the nearest other rank, rather than not at all. Every point of a grid is times its size: 4 beetles ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.4|floatformat:"-2" }}, 9 ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.9|floatformat:"-2" }}, 16 ×{{ v.GAME_GRID_SIZE_FACTOR.16|floatformat:"-2" }}. A photo flagged as bad leaves the grid and counts for nothing; half of them flagged (or the odd one) ends the grid, unscored.

Odd One Out

worth   = odd_weight × P[how related the odd one is to the rest] × size   odd_weight = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}
correct = + worth
wrong   = − odd_wrong × worth                                           odd_wrong = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_WRONG_FACTOR|floatformat:"-2" }}
skip    = + {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_SKIP|floatformat:"-2" }}

A blind guess is correct one time in 4, 9 or 16 and wrong on every validated beetle of the rest, so a wrong pick costing more than a correct one earns makes guessing lose on average at every size (see the checks below). A pick on an unvalidated beetle is scored like a name on it (below): by agreement that it doesn't belong, never negative.

Select all

share  = select_weight × P[the grid's rank] × size ÷ validated members   select_weight = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_SELECT_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}
points = share × ( members tapped − select_wrong × non-members tapped )   select_wrong = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_SELECT_WRONG|floatformat:"-2" }}
members left out cost nothing; taps on unvalidated beetles are recorded, not scored; skip = + {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_SKIP|floatformat:"-2" }}

Members are about a quarter to under half of a grid (4 beetles: 1–2, 9: 3–4, 16: 5–7) and validated non-members are never fewer, so tapping everything loses while wrong taps cost more than a member earns.

In every game

  • Taking part: every real answer earns +{{ v.GAME_POINTS_PARTICIPATION|floatformat:"-2" }} on top, so the score grows with play.
  • Skip in Identification and Similarity costs {{ v.GAME_POINTS_UNSURE|floatformat:"-2" }}; in Odd One Out and Select all it earns {{ v.GAME_POINTS_ODD_SKIP|floatformat:"-2" }}.
  • Seen again: a beetle the player got wrong comes back to be learned and earns ×{{ v.GAME_POINTS_RETRY_FACTOR|floatformat:"-2" }}. Only the first time counts towards accuracy.
  • Reported: while a player's report on a beetle is open, their answers on it are held (no points either way).
  • Score is the running total, never below zero.

Unvalidated beetles: scored by agreement

Here nobody knows the answer yet, so a player earns points when stronger players agree with them. It is never negative, so honest answers on hard beetles are never punished.

for each rank the player named:
  judge weight  = 1                                        an Identification expert for that part of the tree
                = 1 / (1 + e^−(judge rating − your rating) / spread)   otherwise (spread = {{ v.GAME_RATER_SPREAD|floatformat:"-2" }})
  c             = (Σ weight agreeing − Σ weight disagreeing) / (Σ all weights + 1)      from −1 to +1
  points        = cap × P[rank] × max(0, c)                                         cap = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_CONSENSUS_CAP|floatformat:"-2" }}
if Identification experts (or a trusted model) name that rank and you match them:
  points        = max(points, reference_cap × P[rank])                              reference_cap = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_REFERENCE_CAP|floatformat:"-2" }}
(Identification answers then × weight {{ v.GAME_POINTS_CLASSIFY_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }})
  • Judges are players with at least {{ v.GAME_RATER_MIN_JUDGED }} answers on validated beetles and a rating at or above the median. Weaker players never move anyone's points.
  • Agreeing with stronger players earns almost full credit; with weaker ones, very little. A stronger player who disagrees cancels out agreement from weaker ones, so a crowd of weak players can't outvote one strong one.
  • The "+1" in c means one judge alone never gives full credit: the more strong players agree, the closer to the cap.
  • A trusted model counts only when it is at least {{ v.GAME_REF_MODEL_MIN_CONFIDENCE|floatformat:"-2" }} sure of this beetle and has been correct {{ v.GAME_REF_MODEL_MIN_PRECISION|floatformat:"-2" }} of the time on at least {{ v.GAME_REF_MODEL_MIN_CHECKED }} of its sure calls in that same group.
  • These points move as more people answer, and are replaced by the real score once the beetle is validated. They never count towards accuracy or expertise.

Naming confidence: what a beetle is, and what it is not

Points are for players; this is about the beetles. Every answer on an unvalidated beetle is also evidence about its name, rank by rank. Evidence comes in two kinds.

at each rank, for each name given:
  weight(player, rank) = (correct + 1) / (judged + 2)      on validated beetles at that rank: a newcomer counts as a coin flip
  tally[name]          = Σ weight × vote                    (vote = 1, or {{ v.GAME_SELECT_TAP_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }} for a tap)
  support              = tally[leading name] / Σ tally       0 to 1
  • Trusted at a rank: an Identification expert for that part of the tree supports the name and no trusted player disagrees. Taps alone never make a name trusted.
  • Curator tip on the Image Annotation page: the deepest rank that is trusted, or has at least {{ v.GAME_TIP_MIN_VOTES }} players and {{ v.GAME_TIP_MIN_SUPPORT|floatformat:"-2" }} support ("3 reliable players say genus Xyleborus"), flagged when it disagrees with the current label.
  • Written in: when {{ v.GAME_AUTO_APPLY_MIN_EXPERTS }} Identification experts give the same species for a beetle with no species name, it is written in as an Expert ID, still unvalidated for a curator to confirm. Otherwise names only reach a beetle through a curator.
  • The AI adds its own suggestion (shown separately); it becomes a reference for scoring only when proven in that group (see above).

It helps a curator rule names out, and flags a current label that players reject.

What players see

  • After each answer: what players ranked above them said about that beetle, or that they are the first to name it. Only stronger players count, so newcomers learn from better players, not from each other.
  • Grid games: on validated beetles, the answer straight away: the odd one ringed with its name against the rest's ("Odd one: Xylosandrus · the rest: Xyleborus"); in Select all, members found, wrong taps and members missed. This shows what each beetle is and what it is not.
  • Round review and "Where I lost points": the real name of each validated beetle and, rank by rank, where an answer went wrong.
  • Reviewed by curators: when a beetle they answered is validated later, its real name and how their points moved.
  • Players are never told which beetles are validated before they answer, and never see the votes of players below them.

Reliability, levels and experts

  • Reliability (rating) is the lower end of a Wilson interval on how often the player is correct, rank by rank, the first time they see each validated beetle. A few lucky answers don't make anyone an authority: the rating rises only with many correct answers. A Select all grid counts once, as correct only when perfect.
  • Levels need points and a rating (none at levels 1–2, 35% at 3, rising to 92% at the top), so the top levels are kept only while answers stay good.
  • Identification experts in a taxon have answered {{ v.GAME_TRUST_IMAGES_PER_SPECIES }} validated images each of at least {{ v.GAME_TRUST_CHILDREN_SHARE|floatformat:"-2" }} of its members (a genus's species, a tribe's genera, a subfamily's tribes; at least {{ v.GAME_TRUST_MIN_JUDGED }} answers in all) and been correct at least {{ v.GAME_TRUST_MIN_ACCURACY|floatformat:"-2" }} of the time, and are among the top {{ v.GAME_EXPERT_PERCENTILE|floatformat:"-2" }} of players by rating. Their agreeing labels go to curators as trusted suggestions; when two such experts agree on a beetle with no name, it is written in as an Expert ID, still unvalidated for a curator to confirm.
  • Distinction experts reach the same coverage and accuracy telling a taxon's members apart in Similarity, Odd One Out and Select all (each judged answer covers the members it showed). It is shown on their expertise tree and profile only: it never makes a label trusted, weighs as a judge or writes anything in.

Difficulty: which beetles a player sees, and what they are worth

Which beetles

Each beetle has a difficulty from 0 (easy) to 1 (hard): 1 − IBBI-AI's confidence, or else how often players get it wrong. Beetles are picked near the player's target:

target = start + per_batch × finished batches + skill_weight × rating     (at most max)
in Identification and Similarity, over the player's last {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_RECENT }} answers on validated beetles in that game:
  share correct < {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_EASE_BELOW|floatformat:"-2" }}   target − {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_EASE|floatformat:"-2" }}   (never below 0.05)
  share correct > {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_PUSH_ABOVE|floatformat:"-2" }}   target + {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_PUSH|floatformat:"-2" }}   (never above max)
start = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_START|floatformat:"-3" }}   per_batch = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_PER_ROUND|floatformat:"-3" }}   skill_weight = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_SKILL_WEIGHT|floatformat:"-2" }}   max = {{ v.GAME_DIFFICULTY_MAX|floatformat:"-2" }}

The rating moves slowly; the recent answers react within a few batches, so a player who struggles gets a breather and one who is doing well keeps being stretched.

What they are worth (Identification and Similarity)

p    = how hard the beetle is among all playable beetles when answered (0 easiest, 1 hardest; 0.5 if unknown)
m    = 1 + spread × (2p − 1)                                spread = {{ v.GAME_POINTS_DIFFICULTY_SPREAD|floatformat:"-2" }}
gain × m,   loss (skips too) × (2 − m)                       the point for taking part is not scaled

One factor for the whole answer, so a wrong answer never turns into a gain and every comparison between answers on one beetle stays as above. Odd One Out and Select all are priced by their own grid instead. Answers from before this have no p and keep ×1.
